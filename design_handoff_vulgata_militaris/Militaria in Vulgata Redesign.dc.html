Militaria in Vulgata

A research companion to the military lexicon of Jerome's Vulgate. Each chapter is given in its Latin text, with military terms linked to terminology cards that trace every word from the Hebrew (TM) and Greek (LXX) through to the Vulgate rendering.

New here? The Introduction explains the project — the military models of the Hebrew Bible, their Roman re-coding, and how to read the cards.

castra

193 in scope (draft: 148 confirmed as castra, 45 unmapped); 45 in Numbers (noun, full text, verified).

Camp, encampment; by metonymy also “army, host.” Root ḥ-n-h, “to encamp.”

Jerome renders maḥăneh almost exclusively as castra, the technical Roman fortified camp, not a neutral calque of the LXX's more generic παρεμβολή (“encampment, drawing-up”). The verb ḥānâ “to encamp” becomes castrametari, “to lay out a Roman camp” — that card indexes the 39 verb occurrences; this one indexes the 45 noun occurrences.
