# Militaria in Vulgata

A research companion to the military lexicon of Jerome's Vulgate. Each chapter is given in its Latin text, with military terms linked to terminology cards that trace every word from the Hebrew (TM) and Greek (LXX) through to the Vulgate rendering.

New here? The **[[introduction|Introduction]]** explains the project — the military models of the Hebrew Bible, their Roman re-coding, and how to read the cards.

> [!info] Levels of military language (L1–L3)
> Every lexicon card is tagged with one or more of these levels — see the **[[introduction|Introduction]]** for the full discussion:
>
> - **L1** — internal rules of the armed forces (hierarchy, discipline, command, census, camp order, signals)
> - **L2** — conduct of armed conflict (combatants, civilians, prisoners, booty, ḥērem, siege rules)
> - **L3** — use of war and wartime (declarations, embassies, truces, treaties, oaths, surrender, sacred places)

> [!info] Semantic domain (category A–D)
> A separate axis from the levels above — see the **[[introduction|Introduction]]** for the full discussion:
>
> - **A** — weapons and equipment
> - **B** — the army as institution (command, muster, camp, ranks)
> - **C** — actions and legal-abstract outcomes of conflict
> - **D** — the sacral-ritual register of holy war

## Lexicon at a Glance

Grouped by theme; the letter after each heading is the semantic-domain **category** from the legend above (A–D), running here in the same A→D order. Most terms in a group share that category — the rare exception is called out where it occurs; each card also carries its own L1–L3 level.

**Weapons** (A) — [[gladius]] · [[sagitta]] · [[pugio]]

**The camp** (B) — [[castra]] · [[castrametari]] · [[turma]] · [[cuneus]] · [[vexillum]] · [[signum]] · [[praesidium]] · [[custodia]] · [[excubiae]]

**The assembly** (B) — [[multitudo]] · [[synagoga]] · [[congregatio]] — three renderings of one Hebrew word, *ʿēdâ*: the crowd, the official organ, the cultic body (see also [[seditio]] for the same lemma turned hostile)

**Command, census, levy** (B) — [[exercitus]] · [[princeps]] · [[proceres]] · [[dux]] · [[ductor]] · [[tribunus]] · [[centurio]] · [[magister]] · [[recensere]] · [[numerare]] · [[vir fortis]] · [[armatus]] · [[expeditus]] · [[accinctus]]

**Battle and its aftermath** (C) — [[bellum]] · [[proelium]] · [[pugnare]] · [[dimicare]] · [[percutere]] · [[interficere]] · [[occidere]] · [[delere]] · [[vastare]] · [[subvertere]] · [[occupare]] · [[fugere]] · [[persequi]] · [[captivus]] · [[praeda]] · [[victoria]] · [[explorare]] · [[considerare]] · [[insidiae]] · [[seditio]] *(seditio alone is category B here — it follows the assembly it names, not the battle narrative it appears in)*

**The enemy** (C) — [[hostis]] · [[inimicus]]

**War as law and command** (C) — [[foedus]] · [[pactum]] · [[iuramentum]] · [[maledictio]] · [[imperium]] · [[sors]] · [[vindicare]] *(imperium alone is category D — divine command belongs to the sacral register, not the juridical one)*

**Signals and march** (D) — [[tuba]] · [[clangor]] · [[ululatus]] · [[profectio]] *(profectio alone is category B — it names the institutional act of breaking camp, not a signal itself)*

**The sacral register** (D) — [[anathema]] · [[zelus]] · [[ultio]]

**The mantic register** (D) — [[augurium]] · [[divinatio]] · [[ariolus]]
