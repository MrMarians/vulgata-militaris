# victoria

**Victory** — and here is the remarkable thing: in the Hebrew of Numbers there is no word for it. Both Latin occurrences are Jerome's additions.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| — (21,1: the Hebrew has only "fought… and took captives"); תְּרוּעַת מֶלֶךְ — *tərûʿat melek*, "the King's acclaim" (23,21) | — ; τὰ ἔνδοξα ἀρχόντων (23,21) | Sym. σημασία (cf. 23,21) | **victor / victoriae** | 2 in Numbers (both without direct Hebrew counterpart) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,1]] the king of Arad "pugnavit contra illum et **victor** existens duxit ex eo praedam": the Hebrew narrates fight and capture; the *victor* is supplied — Latin war-thinking needs the outcome named. At [[Liber Numerorum/23|Nm 23,21]] the *tərûʿat melek*, the ritual acclaim of YHWH as King (see [[clangor]], [[ululatus]]), becomes "**clangor victoriae** regis": the sacral shout is read as a victory-fanfare — where the LXX had found "the glories of rulers". Biblical Hebrew expresses winning through deliverance and prevailing verbs, not through an abstract noun; *victoria*, goddess and coin-legend of Rome, is precisely the category the translator brings with him. A Roman term with no direct Hebrew counterpart — and, as always, Jerome's own Hebrew text (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic) is unknown: the alignment is ours.
