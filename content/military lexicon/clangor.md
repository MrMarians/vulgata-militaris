# clangor

The blast of the *tuba*, in noun and verb (*clangor*, *clangere*) — a word of epic and martial register, standard in the late-antique pairing *clangor tubarum* (Ammianus: "tubarumque concinente clangore"). Jerome chooses it against the *cano* of the older Latin versions.

## Textual tradition

|---|---|---|---|---|
| תָּקַע / תְּקִיעָה — the sustained blast; תְּרוּעָה — the broken alarm-blast | σαλπίζω / σημασία; at 23,21 τὰ ἔνδοξα ἀρχόντων | Sym. σημασία (cf. 23,21) | **clangor / clangere** (*simplex* vs *concisus*) | 8 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

The Hebrew ordinance distinguishes two executions: the **təqîʿâ**, sustained, for convocation, and the **tərûʿâ**, broken, for departure and battle-alarm. Jerome maps the opposition onto *simplex clangor* versus *prolixior atque concisus clangor* (with [[ululatus]] reserved for the tərûʿâ's howl) — a technical-functional reading of the signal code close in spirit to Vegetius' chapter on the legion's musicians. Two loci stand out: [[Liber Numerorum/23|Nm 23,21]], where the *tərûʿat melek*, the acclamation of YHWH as King, becomes ***clangor victoriae regis*** — the LXX had read "the glories of rulers" (τὰ ἔνδοξα ἀρχόντων) — and [[Liber Numerorum/29|Nm 29,1]], where Yom Təruʿâ is *dies clangoris*.
