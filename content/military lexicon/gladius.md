# gladius

The **sword** — the Roman soldier's weapon par excellence for the all-purpose Hebrew *ḥereb*. Seven occurrences in Numbers, plus two where the sword slips into other words.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חֶרֶב — *ḥereb* | μάχαιρα / ῥομφαία | — | **gladius** | 7 in Numbers (full text, verified; 2 further ḥereb migrate: 19,16 → *occisi*, 20,18 → *armatus*) |

Three clusters. (1) The fear of the sword: the rebels expect to fall *gladio* in Canaan ([[Liber Numerorum/14|Nm 14,3.43]]). (2) The conquest idiom: Israel smites Sihon *in ore gladii* ([[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,24]]) — "at the **mouth** of the sword", a Hebraizing calque of *lə-p̄î ḥereb* that Jerome keeps against classical usage: proof that his Latin can hebraize as deliberately as it romanizes. (3) The drawn sword of the angel in the Balaam episode ([[Liber Numerorum/22|Nm 22,23.31]]), with Balaam's impotent wish for one of his own (22,29) — and the closing of that arc at [[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31,8]], where Balaam himself is killed *gladio*. Two *ḥereb* have migrated into neighbouring families: at 19,16 the "slain of the sword" in the open field is simply *occisi* (see [[occidere]]), and at 20,18 Edom's "with the sword" becomes *armatus* (see [[armatus]]).
