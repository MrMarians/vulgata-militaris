# signum

The **standard** — in Roman usage the ensign of the legionary unit, the object soldiers
rally to. In Numbers the word carries three related loads: the tribal ensign of the camp
order, the memorial-warning sign kept before the sanctuary, and the **pole-standard** on
which the bronze serpent is raised.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| אוֹת — *ʾōt* "sign" (2,2; 16,38; 17,10); נֵס — *nēs* "standard, pole" (21,8.9) | σημέα (2,2); σημεῖον | — | **signum** | 5 in Numbers (military/memorial senses) |

Three senses, one Latin word. (1) In the camp ordinance ([[Liber Numerorum/02|Nm 2,2]]) the
*ʾōtōt* of the ancestral houses become *signa atque vexilla* — see [[vexillum]] for the
hendiadys. (2) The censers of Korah's rebels, hammered into plates for the altar, and
Aaron's rod laid up before the testimony are each a *signum* — a warning ensign planted at
the sanctuary ([[Liber Numerorum/16|Nm 16,38]]; [[Liber Numerorum/17|Nm 17,10]]; note the
versification: these correspond to MT 17,3 and 17,25). (3) The bronze serpent is set *pro
signo* on the pole ([[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,8.9]]): here the Hebrew is *nēs*, the
battle-standard of the divine-war lexicon, and *signum* makes the serpent a military ensign
lifted for the routed to rally to.

The miracle-"signs" of the Exodus (*signis* at 14,11.22, Hebrew *ʾōtōt* in the sense of
wonders) belong to a different semantic field and are deliberately **not** indexed here.
