# tuba

The Roman war-trumpet — the instrument that, in the *castra*, orders the striking of camp ("Vereris, ne tubam non exaudias?", Crassus in Frontinus, *Strat.* I,1,13). In Numbers it renders the two **silver trumpets** hammered for Moses: signal-instrument of convocation, march and war, in priestly hands.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חֲצֹצְרָה — *ḥăṣōṣərâ* | σάλπιγξ | — | **tuba** | 10 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

Numbers never uses *šôp̄ār*, the ram's horn: its only trumpet is the metal *ḥăṣōṣərâ*, and Jerome's *tuba* is its exact institutional match. The ordinance of [[Liber Numerorum/10|Nm 10,1-10]] regulates the signal-code — convocation, alarm, war, festival — and at 10,2 the purpose-clause *quando movenda sunt castra* reproduces the very grammar of the Roman signal manual (Vegetius, *Epit. rei mil.* II,22: "quotiens movenda sunt signa, cornicines canunt"): same gerundive, same castrense subject. At [[Liber Numerorum/29|Nm 29,1]] the feast of the seventh month is *dies clangoris et tubarum* (Yom Təruʿâ); at [[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31,6]] the trumpets go to war in the hands of Phinehas the priest.
