# tribunus

The Roman **tribune** — in the classical legion one of its six senior officers, in late
antiquity the commander of a unit. In Numbers it renders the top grade of Israel's decimal
command ladder: the *śarê hā-ʾălāp̄îm*, the commanders of thousands.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| שָׂרֵי הָאֲלָפִים — *śarê hā-ʾălāp̄îm* | χιλίαρχος | — | **tribunus** | 3 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

All three occurrences belong to the **review after the Midian war**
([[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31]]): the *tribuni et centuriones* muster the troops, report that
not one man is missing, and offer the gold of the spoil. The equivalence χιλίαρχος →
*tribunus* is not Jerome's coinage: the whole four-grade ladder (*tribunus / centurio /
quinquagenarius / decanus*, Ex 18,21.25; Dt 1,15) is already in the Old Latin — a piece of
Romanisation the Vulgate **inherits and keeps** rather than invents. In Numbers only the two
upper grades appear, and only in ch. 31: the census chapters prefer the tribal titles
([[princeps]], [[dux]]).
