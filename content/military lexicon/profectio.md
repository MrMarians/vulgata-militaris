# profectio

The **ceremonial departure** — in imperial Rome the *profectio Augusti* is the emperor's solemn setting-out for war, celebrated on a dedicated coin-type (PROFECTIO AVG, from Trajan onward) and codified in the panegyrics; in the military prose it is the army's tuba-signalled departure ("signo profectionis dato", Caesar, *BC* III,76).

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| מַסַּע — *massaʿ*, "breaking of camp, stage" (root nsʿ) | ἀπαρτία (10,12, "baggage"); στρατιαί (10,28) | οἱ Γʹ: ἀπάρσεις (cf. Ep. 78,1) | **profectio** | 2 in Numbers (noun; the verb *proficisci* ~24×) |

The Hebrew *masʿê* are the "pullings-up" of the tent-pegs, the stages of the desert route. The Greek tradition wavers — the LXX turns them into "baggage" at 10,12 and into "expeditions" (στρατιαί) at 10,28, while the hexaplaric versions have ἀπάρσεις, the very word Jerome cites in Ep. 78,1: "quas Graeci ἀπάρσεις vocant, nos… significantius 'mansiones' sive, quia de exercitu dicitur, 'castra' transtulimus." Choosing *profectio* activates the Roman ceremonial register: the stages of the wilderness become opening ceremonies of a divine campaign. A Roman technicism with no direct Hebrew counterpart — the alignment (and Jerome's Hebrew text, unvocalized and possibly non-Masoretic) remains ours, not his. The ordinary verb *proficisci* ("to set out", ~24 occurrences across the book) carries the narrative traffic and is not indexed separately.
