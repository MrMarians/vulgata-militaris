# sors

The **lot** — the sacral-juridical decision device. Rome assigned provinces and commands *sorte*, by the drawing of lots; Israel divides the promised land the same way, and the Vulgate's word for it is exactly the Roman one.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| גּוֹרָל — *gôrāl*; at 34,2 the land that "falls" (*nāp̄al*) to Israel | κλῆρος (with κληρονομία for the inheritance) | — | **sors** | 8 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

The land-law is fixed at the second census: divide "**sorte**… by the lot" ([[Liber Numerorum/26|Nm 26,55.56]]) — and the Hebrew of v. 56 says the division happens "at the **mouth** of the lot" (*ʿal-pî hag-gôrāl*), the very idiom that elsewhere gives God's *imperium* (see [[imperium]]): the lot speaks, and its word binds. The command returns in the conquest ordinance ([[Liber Numerorum/33|Nm 33,54]], twice) and the boundary charter ([[Liber Numerorum/34|Nm 34,2]] — where "the land that shall **fall** to you" is explicitated as *sorte ceciderit* — and 34,13). The book closes on it: the case of Zelophehad's daughters turns on land assigned *sorte* ([[Liber Numerorum/36|Nm 36,2]]) and on keeping the *sortium distributio* intact across the jubilee (36,4). War's end-state in Numbers is not triumph but **allotment**: the lot is how conquest becomes law.
