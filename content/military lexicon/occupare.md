# occupare

To **occupy** — the verb of military seizure and, in Roman usage, of occupation as a legal
fact: taking possession. One occurrence in Numbers.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| לכד — *lākad*, "to capture" | ἔλαβεν | — | **occupavit** | 1 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

[[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32,41]]: "Iair… abiit et **occupavit** viculos eius" — Jair takes
the tent-villages of Gilead and renames them Havvoth-Jair. Of the three renderings *lākad*
receives in the conquest notices of ch. 32 (capture → *vastare* at v. 39, capture kept
plain elsewhere), *occupare* is the most juridical: not the storming but the resulting
**possession**, sealed by the act of renaming. A small window on how the Vulgate's conquest
vocabulary slides between violence and title-taking.
