# expeditus

The **light-ready soldier** — in Caesar's prose the *expediti* are the troops freed of
baggage for rapid action. Three occurrences, all rendering the same Hebrew *ḥālûṣ* that
elsewhere becomes [[armatus]] and [[accinctus]]: one root, three Roman nuances.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חֲלוּצֵי (הַצָּבָא) — *ḥălûṣê (haṣ-ṣābāʾ)*, "the equipped of the host" | ἐνωπλισμένοι (regularly) | — | **expeditus** | 3 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31,5]] the thousand men per tribe delivered for the Midian war
are *expeditorum ad pugnam* — a picked strike-force, not the full levy. In the Transjordan
compact ([[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32,20.27]]) Gad and Ruben promise to cross the Jordan
*expediti* — travelling light, families and herds left behind: exactly the classical sense
of soldiers stripped for the campaign. Where *armatus* stresses the weapons and *accinctus*
the girding, *expeditus* stresses **readiness of movement**: three Latin lights on a single
Hebrew word, and as always the choice among them is Jerome's — read against a Hebrew text
we no longer possess as he had it.
