# accinctus

**Girded** — *accingere* is the gesture of battle-readiness itself, the sword-belt drawn tight. A hapax in Numbers, inside a doubled formula.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חֻשִׁים — *ḥušîm*, "in haste / ready" (a rare form) | ἐνοπλισάμενοι προφυλακή ("armed as a vanguard") | — | **armati et accincti** | 1 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32,17]] Gad and Ruben pledge: "nos autem ipsi **armati et accincti** pergemus ad proelium ante filios Israhel." The Hebrew has the levy verb plus the rare *ḥušîm*, "swiftly/ready"; the Greek read a vanguard (προφυλακή); Jerome unfolds the phrase into a Latin **pair** — weapons plus sword-belt — that makes the pledge sound like a soldier's oath of readiness. The doubling has no separate Hebrew trigger, and since the Hebrew text Jerome read is unknown (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic — precisely the kind of rare form where readings diverge), the alignment is ours, not his. Same root-family as the *ḥālûṣ* of [[armatus]] and [[expeditus]]: one Hebrew idea of battle-readiness, three Latin registers.
