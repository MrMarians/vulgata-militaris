# armatus

**Armed** — the participle-adjective of the man under arms. Six occurrences, five of them
for the Hebrew *ḥālûṣ*, the man "equipped/stripped for battle" — the vanguard word of the
Transjordan compact — and one striking substitution.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חלץ — *ḥālaṣ/ḥālûṣ* (31,3; 32,17.21.29.32); חֶרֶב — *ḥereb* "sword" (20,18) | ἐνοπλίζομαι / ἐνωπλισμένοι; at 20,18 ἐν πολέμῳ | — | **armatus / armare** | 6 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

*Ḥālaṣ* is one Hebrew root that Jerome refracts into **three** Latin words — *armatus*,
[[expeditus]], [[accinctus]] — the mirror-image of this index's method (one Latin word,
several Hebrew ones). The theological loci are the Transjordan negotiations
([[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32]]): Gad and Ruben may keep their land only *if you go armed
before the LORD to war*. At [[Liber Numerorum/20|Nm 20,18]] the substitution: Edom's threat
"lest I come out against you **with the sword**" (*ba-ḥereb*) — "in war" for the LXX —
becomes "**armatus** occurram tibi": the weapon becomes the armed man. At
[[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31,3]] the mobilization order for the Midian war: "armate ex vobis
viros ad pugnam."

## Loci (all occurrences in Numbers)

- [[Liber Numerorum/20|Nm 20]]: v. 18 — Edom's armed refusal (Hebrew: "with the sword")
- [[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31]]: v. 3 — the mobilization order
