# divinatio

**Divination** — the generic Latin term for the mantic art as such (Cicero wrote a *De divinatione* to survey it). Two occurrences, and together with [[augurium]] they translate the Hebrew Bible's merism for the whole field of forbidden foreknowledge.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| קֶסֶם — *qesem*, the technical-professional divination | τὰ μαντεῖα (22,7); μαντεία (23,23) | — | **divinatio** | 2 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

*Qesem* is the diviner's craft as a **trade**: instrumental, professional, typically foreign, the practice Ezekiel brands as the mark of false prophets. Numbers shows the trade at work: the elders of Moab travel to Balaam carrying "**divinationis** pretium" ([[Liber Numerorum/22|Nm 22,7]]) — the fee in their hands, prophecy as a purchasable weapon. Jerome the exegete made exactly this the dividing line: commenting on Micah he anchors the difference between *prophetia* and *divinatio* to our verse — "numquam enim divinatio in scripturis in bonam partem accipitur… Non erit augurium in Iacob neque divinatio in Israel" — the diviner is the prophet who takes money. At [[Liber Numerorum/23|Nm 23,23]] the pair *augurium / divinatio* (Hebrew *naḥaš / qesem*, a merism found again at Dt 18,10 and 2 Kgs 17,17) denies Israel the entire mantic economy: no omens, no fees, no hired seers — the war-oracles of this people are not for sale.
