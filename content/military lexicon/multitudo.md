# multitudo

The **default rendering** of *ʿēdâ* — not the official organ ([[synagoga]]) nor the cultic body ([[congregatio]]), but the physical mass: the crowd that gathers, marches, and above all *murmurs*. Where the LXX reads every *ʿēdâ* through a single mechanical equivalent (συναγωγή), Jerome makes *multitudo* the unmarked term for the community as sheer aggregate — some thirty-six occurrences, by far the most frequent of the three.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| עֵדָה — *ʿēdâ*, "the assembly" (root *yʿd*, "to appoint a meeting") | συναγωγή (mechanical, regardless of referent); Peshitta and Targumim uniformly *kᵉništā* | — | **multitudo** (default; contrast [[synagoga]], [[congregatio]]) | ca. 36 in Numbers *(loci below; cf. [[synagoga]] and [[congregatio]] for the remaining distribution)* |

Jerome dissolves the LXX's one-to-one equivalence into *variatio*: the same Hebrew noun becomes *multitudo* whenever the accent falls on the community as countable, physical crowd — often armed, often complaining. The census formulas of chs. 1 and 26 count it; the murmuring narratives of chs. 14–16 stage it in revolt; Phinehas's zeal in [[Liber Numerorum/25|Nm 25]] is provoked by what the *multitudo* is doing at the tent door. The choice matters precisely because it is the *unmarked* member of the set: *multitudo* is what *ʿēdâ* is by default, before it acts officially (→ [[synagoga]]) or stands before the Lord as a cultic body (→ [[congregatio]]) — or, in three sharply negative moments, is read as outright mutiny (see [[seditio]]). The *variatio* is wider still: elsewhere the same referent surfaces as *turba*, *populus*/*plebs*, *coetus*, *concilium*, *globus*, *vulgus*, or *frequentia*, and in about a tenth of the occurrences *ʿēdâ* is simply absorbed into the verb or into "filii Israhel"/*omnes*. *Multitudo* is the center of gravity of that whole cloud of renderings, not a term chosen for any one of them.
