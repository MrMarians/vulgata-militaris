# turma

The Roman **cavalry squadron** — thirty riders in Varro (*Ling.* V,91), thirty-two under a *decurio* in Vegetius (*Epit. rei mil.* II,14). Jerome applies it, 17 times, to the tribal divisions of Israel: a people that in the Hebrew text possesses **no cavalry at all** is organized *per turmas*, in squadrons.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| דֶּגֶל — *degel*, "standard; (metonymy) the unit under it"; also לְצִבְאֹתָם *ləṣibʾōtām* "by their hosts" | τάγμα (2,2; 10,14ss) / ἡγεμονία (1,52) | Aq., Sym. τάγμα (ad 2,17) | **turma** | 17 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

*Degel* is properly the tribal **standard** and, by metonymy, the group that marches under it — a genealogical unit defined by an emblem, not by tactics. The LXX takes a first step toward militarization (τάγμα, the Hellenistic "formation"), and the Aramaic tradition goes further: Targum Onkelos renders *degel* with *ṭiqsēh*, a loan of Greek τάξις, and in the Elephantine papyri *degel* is already the technical word for the **unit** of the Jewish garrison. Jerome inherits that militarized frame but chooses the specifically **Roman** tactical unit, the cavalry squadron of the imperial army. In [[Liber Numerorum/01|Nm 1,52]] the Latin triplet *per turmas et cuneos atque exercitum* answers the Hebrew triplet *maḥăneh / degel / ṣəbāʾôt* as a block, so the word-to-word alignment there is ours, not his — and, as always, what Hebrew text Jerome had before him is unknown (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic). A Roman technicism with no direct Hebrew counterpart: no biblical word means "squadron of horse."
