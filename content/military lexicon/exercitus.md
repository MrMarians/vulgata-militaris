# exercitus

An organized force that "goes out" (*yāṣāʾ ṣābāʾ*): the tribal host/army (Nm 1,3), the cosmic host (*ṣəbāʾ haššāmayim*; *YHWH ṣəbāʾôt*), and cultic service (the Levites' *ṣābāʾ*, Nm 4). Varro derived the Latin word from drill — "exercitus, quod exercitando fit melior" — and Servius defined it as an *ordinata multitudo*: the ordered mass, which is exactly what the Vulgate's Numbers makes of Israel.

One Hebrew noun, several Latin faces even within a single chapter: Numbers 1 renders it as *exercitus* (v. 52) and, in the repeated census formula "omnes qui poterant ad bella procedere," effectively as *bellum* (vv. 20-45; see [[bellum]], and [[pugnare]] for ch. 2's *summa pugnatorum*) — *ṣābāʾ* "the muster" read through the lens of what the muster is for. Marked loci within the Numbers distribution: at 1,16 the chiefs are "**capita exercitus** Israhel" — an expression with no real Latin parallel, coined to carry the Hebrew "heads of the thousands"; at [[Liber Numerorum/10|Nm 10,36]] the Ark-prayer addresses the "**multitudinem exercitus** Israhel" (Hebrew "the myriads of the thousands"), the pairing Servius' definition almost predicts; and at [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,23]] Sihon's **people** (*ʿam*) marches out as an *exercitu congregato* — even the enemy's populace is militarized in translation. *Militia* (outside this book) reads the host as **military service**, the sense that feeds *militia Christi* in the Christian reception; Numbers itself never uses *miles* or *militia* — its army is *exercitus*, *turmae*, *castra*.
