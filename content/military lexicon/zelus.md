# zelus

**Zeal** — the Greek loan ζῆλος naturalized in Latin. One Hebrew root, *qnʾ*, covers both the husband's jealousy and God's consuming zeal; the Vulgate of Numbers splits it into two Latin words, and the split is itself an interpretation.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| קנא — *qānāʾ* verb, *qinʾâ* noun | ζῆλος / ζηλόω; πνεῦμα ζηλώσεως (ch. 5) | — | **zelotypia** (marital, ch. 5) / **zelus, zelari** (divine, ch. 25) | 9 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

In the ordeal of [[Liber Numerorum/05|Nm 5]] the *rûaḥ qinʾâ*, the spirit of jealousy that seizes the husband, is rendered six times with the technical Greek-derived **zelotypia** — jealousy as a pathology with a legal procedure attached (*lex zelotypiae*, 5,29). In [[Liber Numerorum/25|Nm 25]] the same root becomes **zelus**: Phinehas turns back God's wrath "quia **zelo** meo commotus est… ut non ipse delerem filios Israhel in **zelo** meo" (25,11 — the Hebrew doubles it, "when he zealed my zeal") and is rewarded "quia **zelatus** est pro Deo suo" (25,13). The passion that in a husband is suspicion, in the priest is the very jealousy of God enacted — violence transmuted into covenant (the *foedus pacis* of 25,12, the *pactum sacerdotii* of 25,13; the weapon is a [[pugio]]). This is the "Phinehas complex" at the root of later zealotry: 1 Macc 2,26 will praise Mattathias precisely for zeal "as Phinehas did." One root, two Latin registers — and the register-choice, like everything here, is the translator's, made against a Hebrew text (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic) that we no longer possess as he had it.
