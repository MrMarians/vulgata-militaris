# maledictio

The **curse** — and in Numbers the curse is a weapon system: Balak, unable to field an army against Israel, hires a professional to curse it. Twenty-one occurrences in two theatres: the ordeal of ch. 5 and the Balaam cycle.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ארר *ʾārar* (the operative curse); קבב *qābab* (Balaam's verb); אָלָה *ʾālâ* (the oath-curse); זעם *zāʿam* → *detestari* (the exception) | ἀράομαι / καταράομαι / ἐπικατάρατος | — | **maledicere / maledictio** | 21 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

Hebrew possesses a taxonomy of cursing; the Latin family flattens it — with one telling exception. In the sotah ordeal ([[Liber Numerorum/05|Nm 5]], nine occurrences) the *ʾālâ*, the oath-curse the woman swallows, becomes *aquae maledictae*, *maledictio*, and the written curses the priest blots out (v. 23, see [[delere]]): the curse as judicial instrument. In the Balaam cycle (chh. 22-24, twelve occurrences) Balak's commission uses *ʾārar* ("maledic mihi populo huic", 22,6) while Balaam's own refusals use his technical verb *qābab* ("quomodo **maledicam** cui non **maledixit** Deus?", [[Liber Numerorum/23|Nm 23,8]]) — both go to *maledicere*; only *zāʿam*, "to execrate", keeps a verb of its own (*detestari*, same verse). The cycle closes on the boomerang formula: "**maledictus** qui maledixerit tibi" ([[Liber Numerorum/24|Nm 24,9]]) — the curse-weapon that returns upon its user, and the theological reason the hired curse must fail (cf. [[augurium]], [[divinatio]] for the mantic frame).
