# dux

The **commander** — the most military of the leadership titles, and in the Vulgate of
Numbers the minority partner of [[princeps]] in a deliberate *variatio*: the same Hebrew
*nāśîʾ* is *dux* in the offering-list of 7,3-18 and *princeps* from 7,24 on.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| נָשִׂיא — *nāśîʾ* (7; 10,24; 25,14.18; 34,26); רֹאשׁ — *rōʾš* "head" (14,4); נְדִיבֵי הָעָם "nobles of the people" (21,18); פַּאֲתֵי "temples/flanks (of Moab)" (24,17) | ἄρχων / ἀρχηγός (14,4 ἀρχηγόν; 24,17 ἀρχηγούς) | — | **dux** | 11 in Numbers (full text, verified; *duces* = verb "you shall lead" at 11,16 and 20,25, excluded) |

Three loci carry theological weight. At [[Liber Numerorum/14|Nm 14,4]] the faithless people
say "constituamus nobis **ducem** et revertamur in Aegyptum" — the counter-exodus gets its
own commander (Hebrew "let us appoint a *head*", LXX ἀρχηγόν). At
[[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,18]] the well-song's "nobles of the people" become *duces
multitudinis*. And at [[Liber Numerorum/24|Nm 24,17]] the star-oracle's *percutiet **duces**
Moab* follows the LXX (ἀρχηγούς) in reading Moab's "temples/flanks" (*paʾătê*) as its
chiefs — a rendering the messianic tradition made canonical. As throughout, Jerome's Hebrew
text is unknown (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic): the alignments are ours.
