# synagoga

Where [[multitudo]] names the crowd as mass, *synagoga* names it as **institution**: the same *ʿēdâ*, but taken as the official, representative body — almost always in construct with its leadership (*principes synagogae*, *proceres synagogae*). It is the one Latin rendering that keeps faith with the LXX's own mechanical choice (*ʿēdâ* → συναγωγή throughout), yet Jerome does not extend it to the whole range the Greek covers: he reserves it for the deliberative and official register alone.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| עֵדָה — *ʿēdâ*, "the assembly" acting as a constituted, representative body | συναγωγή (the LXX's regular, unmarked equivalent for *ʿēdâ*) | — | **synagoga** (marked: contrast the default [[multitudo]]) | 4 loci documented (below); contrast qāhāl → ἐκκλησία, which the LXX tends toward but not absolutely |

The irony is structural: what is the Greek's *default* becomes, in the Vulgate, the *marked* choice — reserved for the assembly's official and representative capacity, regularly paired with its leaders. At [[Liber Numerorum/04|Nm 4,34]] and [[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31,13]] it is the *principes synagogae* who act; at [[Liber Numerorum/16|Nm 16,2]] the rebels against Moses are *proceres synagogae* — aristocrats of the assembly, not simply a mob (contrast [[seditio]] for the moment their gathering turns hostile); at [[Liber Numerorum/27|Nm 27,20]] it is *omnis synagoga filiorum Israhel* that must obey Joshua, the assembly as the addressee of a formal transfer of authority. Historically this is the term that will come to name the place where rabbinic Judaism gathers — which sharpens, rather than softens, Jerome's restraint in using it: by not letting *synagoga* absorb the whole semantic range of *ʿēdâ*, and by developing [[congregatio]] alongside it for the cultic register, the Vulgate leaves room for the *synagoga* / *congregatio* pair to be read later along confessional lines — the one increasingly marked as Jewish, the other open to the *ecclesia*.
