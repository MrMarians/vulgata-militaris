# insidiae

The **ambush** — war by deception, the *insidiae* of Roman military and legal prose. Three
occurrences, three different treacheries.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| נֵכֶל — *nēḵel* "deceit" (25,18); no direct trigger (32,17); צְדִיָּה — *ṣədiyyâ* "premeditation" (35,20) | ἐν δολιότητι (25,18); ἐξ ἐνέδρου (35,20) | — | **insidiae** | 3 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/25|Nm 25,18]] the Midianite offensive at Peor is defined: "decepere
**insidiis**" — the Hebrew *nēḵel*, deceit, becomes the ambush; the war that follows
(Nm 31, see [[ultio]]) is retribution for a stratagem, not a battle. War by seduction is
still war. At [[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32,17]] the fortified towns protect the families
"propter habitatorum **insidias**" — the Hebrew says only "because of the inhabitants":
Jerome makes the natives ambushers, and the caveat applies (his Hebrew text, unvocalized
and possibly non-Masoretic, is unknown). At [[Liber Numerorum/35|Nm 35,20]] the word turns
juridical: to strike *per insidias* — Hebrew *ṣədiyyâ*, "lying in wait", LXX ἐξ ἐνέδρου —
is what separates the murderer from the manslayer: **premeditation defined as ambush**, the
battlefield category imported into homicide law.
