# magister

The **officer-registrar**. The Hebrew *šōṭēr* (root *šṭr*, cognate with Akkadian words for
writing) is the levy's clerk — the official who keeps the muster-rolls and, in Deuteronomy's
law of war, addresses the troops before battle. One occurrence in Numbers.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| שֹׁטְרָיו — *šōṭərāyw*, "its officers/scribes" | γραμματεῖς ("scribes") | — | **magistri** | 1 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/11|Nm 11,16]] the seventy elders are chosen from those Moses knows to
be "senes populi **ac magistri**" — elders *and officers*. The LXX keeps the clerical sense
(γραμματεῖς); Jerome's *magister* is broader — teacher, master, but also, in the Latin of
his own day, the summit of the army list (*magister militum*, *magister equitum*). The one
Numbers occurrence is administrative rather than tactical, and the card claims no more; but
the choice quietly moves the muster-clerk up the Roman ladder, and elsewhere in the
Heptateuch the same *šōṭərîm* oscillate between scribes and commanders. What Hebrew text
Jerome read is, as always, unknown; the alignment is ours.
