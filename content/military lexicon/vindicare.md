# vindicare

To **claim** — and to **avenge**. *Vindicare* is one of Roman law's most loaded verbs: the *vindicatio* is the formal claim of a right or a thing as one's own, and the same verb serves the avenging of a wrong. The two occurrences in Numbers land exactly on the two senses.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| בקשׁ — *biqqēš* "to seek" (16,10); מוֹת יוּמָת — "he shall surely be put to death" (35,18) | ζητεῖτε ("you seek", 16,10); θανάτῳ θανατούσθω | — | **vindicare** | 2 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/16|Nm 16,10]] Moses accuses Korah's Levites: "ut vobis etiam sacerdotium **vindicetis**" — the Hebrew says they *seek* the priesthood; the Latin makes it a *vindicatio*, a formal claim of title. The mutiny (see [[seditio]], [[proceres]]) is framed as usurpative litigation: they are suing for the altar. At [[Liber Numerorum/35|Nm 35,18]] the other sense: the murderer "percussoris sanguine **vindicabitur**" — where the Hebrew pronounces a court's death-sentence ("he shall surely be put to death"), the Latin phrases it as blood-satisfaction, the wrong avenged in the striker's own blood — the idiom of the [[ultio]] system (the *ultor sanguinis* enters at v. 19). Claim and vengeance, the two faces of *vindicare*, distributed over rebellion and homicide; and as always, the Hebrew text Jerome actually read (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic) is unknown — the alignments are ours.
