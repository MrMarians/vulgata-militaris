# ultio

**Vengeance** — *ultio*, *ulcisci*, and the agent-noun *ultor*, a word with imperial resonance: Augustus built Mars **Ultor** a temple for the avenging of Caesar. Six occurrences that run from the charter of a war to the statute of the blood-avenger.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| נקם — *nāqam/nəqāmâ* (31,2.3); גֹּאֵל הַדָּם — "the redeemer of blood" (35,25.27); שְׁפָטִים — "judgments" (33,4); תְּנוּאָה — "displeasure" (14,34) | ἐκδίκησις / ἐκδικέω; ὁ ἀγχιστεύων τὸ αἷμα (ch. 35) | — | **ultio / ulcisci / ultor** | 6 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

The Midian war is *named* by this word: "**ulciscere** prius filios Israhel de Madianitis" and "qui possint **ultionem** Domini expetere" ([[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31,2.3]] — Hebrew *niqmat-YHWH*, the LORD's vengeance, the *casus belli* for the deception of Peor, see [[insidiae]]). Retrospectively the Exodus itself is re-read in the same key: on the gods of Egypt God "exercuerat **ultionem**" ([[Liber Numerorum/33|Nm 33,4]]) — where the Hebrew has the juridical "executed **judgments**" (*šəp̄āṭîm*), the Latin makes it vengeance. So too at [[Liber Numerorum/14|Nm 14,34]]: "scietis **ultionem** meam" for the rare *tənûʾâ*, God's "displeasure" hardened into revenge. In the homicide law the word turns institutional: the *gōʾēl had-dām*, the kinsman-redeemer of blood (LXX "the one who acts as next-of-kin for the blood"), appears as the "**ultor** sanguinis" ([[Liber Numerorum/35|Nm 35,27]]; *ultoris* 35,25) — alongside the alternative rendering *propinquus occisi* (see [[occidere]]): kinship-obligation recast as the Roman avenger. See [[vindicare]] for the family's juridical twin.
