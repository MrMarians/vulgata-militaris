# proceres

The **grandees** — a hapax in Numbers, and a pointed one. Everywhere else the tribal chiefs
are *principes* (72 times); only for the two hundred and fifty leaders of **Korah's revolt**
does Jerome reach for the epic-aristocratic register: "viri **proceres** synagogae"
([[Liber Numerorum/16|Nm 16,2]]).

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| נְשִׂיאֵי עֵדָה קְרִאֵי מוֹעֵד — "chieftains of the community, summoned of the assembly" | ἀρχηγοὶ συναγωγῆς, σύγκλητοι βουλῆς | — | **proceres** | 1 in Numbers (hapax; *procerae* 13,32 = the adjective "tall", excluded) |

The Hebrew titles of 16,2 are the same honorifics the census chapters render with
*princeps*: the lexical break is entirely Jerome's. Servius preserves Varro's etymology:
the *proceres* are named from the *mutuli*, the beam-heads (*capita trabium*) that jut out
under a roof-line — the "prominent men". By breaking the formula *principes synagogae*
(so 4,34; 31,13) precisely here, the Vulgate stages the revolt as an aristocratic *coup
d'état*: not officers failing in duty but magnates rising against the constitution of the
camp. The same word carried a personal edge for Jerome, who elsewhere imagines the
*proceres* and the *turba patricia* of Rome thundering against him (Ep. 54,2).
