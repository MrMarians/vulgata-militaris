# delere

To **wipe out** — Latin's verb of erasure, from writing-tablets to cities. Seven occurrences, one of which completes the de-sacralisation of the ban.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חרם — *ḥāram* (21,2); מחה *māḥâ* "blot out" (5,23); כרת *kārat* "cut off" (15,31); כלה *kālâ* "consume" (16,45; 25,11); לחך *lāḥak* "lick up" (22,4); גוע *gāwaʿ* "perish" (17,13) | 21,2 ἀναθεματιῶ; 5,23 ἐξαλείψει; ἐξαναλίσκω | — | **delere** | 7 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,2]] Israel vows the ban on Arad — "**delebo** urbes eius" — where the Hebrew has *ḥāram* and the LXX keeps the sacral ἀναθεματιῶ: with [[interficere]] at 21,3, this is the ban's **act** made ordinary destruction while its object keeps the sacral name ([[anathema]]). The other faces of the verb map its literal core, erasure: the priest **blots out** the written curses into the bitter water ([[Liber Numerorum/05|Nm 5,23]] — *delere* at its most exact, of ink); the deliberate sinner "shall be **wiped out** from his people" (15,31); God threatens to **consume** the congregation (16,45; cf. 25,11, the wrath Phinehas' zeal averts); Moab fears Israel will lick the land bare "as the ox licks grass" (22,4); and the terrified people ask whether all must be *delendi* "usque ad internicionem" (17,13) — the extermination-noun that elsewhere renders the ban's paronomasia (Dt 7,2). What Hebrew text Jerome read is unknown; the alignments are ours.
