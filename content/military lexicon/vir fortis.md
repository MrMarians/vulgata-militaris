# vir fortis

The **able-bodied recruit** — *vir fortis* belongs to the lexicon of the Roman *dilectus*,
the levy: Caesar's hand-picked *viri fortes* summoned *nominatim*, Livy's fixed pair *fortis
ac strenuus* for the fit candidate, Vegetius' rule that "utilius est fortes milites esse
quam grandes", and Ammianus — Jerome's contemporary — where *vir fortis* rings through the
generals' harangues ("adeste, viri fortes") and the *dilectus validae iuventutis* imposed on
the defeated.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| כָּל־יֹצֵא צָבָא — "everyone going out to the host" | πᾶς ὁ ἐκπορευόμενος ἐν δυνάμει | — | **omnium virorum fortium** | 1 in Numbers (the iunctura; full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/01|Nm 1,3]] the census criterion — "from twenty years and up, everyone
who goes out to the host" — becomes "omnium **virorum fortium** ex Israhel". The Hebrew
says nothing about strength: it defines eligibility by the act of going out. The Old Latin
had kept that plain ("omnis egrediens ad praeliandum"); Jerome recruits instead the
technical idiom of the levy, turning a census formula into a *dilectus militaris*. A Roman
technicism with no direct Hebrew counterpart — and since his Hebrew text is unknown
(unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic), the alignment is ours, not his. The *fortissimi* of
[[Liber Numerorum/13|Nm 13,28]] (the Canaanites' strong settlers) are the ordinary
adjective, not this iunctura, and are not indexed.
