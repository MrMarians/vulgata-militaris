# centurio

The **centurion** — the professional backbone of the Roman army, and the one Roman rank
every reader of the Gospels knows by name. In Numbers it renders the second grade of the
decimal ladder: the *śarê ham-mēʾôt*, commanders of hundreds.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| שָׂרֵי הַמֵּאוֹת — *śarê ham-mēʾôt* | ἑκατόνταρχος | — | **centurio** | 3 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

Like [[tribunus]], the rendering continues the Old Latin's officer grid (ἑκατόνταρχος →
*centurio* already at Ex 18): inherited Romanisation, faithfully kept. The three Numbers
occurrences all stand in the Midian-war review ([[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31]]), where
*tribuni centurionesque* function as one collegial body — mustering the men, accounting for
the losses (none), and dedicating the officers' gold. Through the Gospels' centurions and
the Church's later reading of them, this is among the most consequential words the military
lexicon of the Latin Bible bequeathed to Christian imagination.
