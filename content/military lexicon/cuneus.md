# cuneus

The **wedge** — the attack formation of Roman infantry, "a mass of foot that starts narrow
and widens as it advances, breaking the enemy line; the soldiers call it *caput porcinum*,
the boar's head" (Vegetius, *Epit. rei mil.* III,19; cf. Servius ad *Aen.* XII,457, Tacitus
*Germ.* 6,6). In Numbers it is an **absolute hapax**: one occurrence, in the camp ordinance.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| דֶּגֶל — *degel* (within the triplet of 1,52) | ἡγεμονία (1,52) | — | **cuneus** | 1 in Numbers (hapax) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/01|Nm 1,52]] — "metabuntur autem castra filii Israhel unusquisque per
turmas et **cuneos** atque exercitum suum" — the Latin triplet *turmae / cunei / exercitus*
answers the Hebrew triplet *maḥăneh / degel / ṣəbāʾôt* as a block. Nothing in the Hebrew
means "wedge": *degel* is a standard and, by metonymy, the unit camped under it; the LXX
here has the colourless ἡγεμονία ("command"). The wedge is pure cultural *translatio* — a
tactical figure of the late-Roman battlefield (Ammianus uses it repeatedly, *cuneatim*)
projected onto the tribal order of Israel. What Hebrew text Jerome read here is unknown
(unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic), so the alignment is ours, not his.
