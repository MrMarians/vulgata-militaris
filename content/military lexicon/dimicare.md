# dimicare

To **fight it out** — in Caesar's idiom the decisive engagement (*proelio dimicare*). A
hapax in Numbers, placed in the one law that binds trumpet, war and divine remembrance
together.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| הַצַּר הַצֹּרֵר — *haṣ-ṣar haṣ-ṣōrēr*, "the foe who presses you" (root ṣrr) | τοὺς ὑπεναντίους ("the adversaries") | — | **qui dimicant adversum vos** | 1 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

[[Liber Numerorum/10|Nm 10,9]]: "si exieritis ad bellum de terra vestra contra hostes **qui
dimicant** adversum vos, clangetis ululantibus tubis…" The Hebrew doubles the root —
"the oppressor who oppresses you" — and Jerome resolves the figura etymologica into the
combat verb: the foe is defined by the fact that he *engages*. The verse is the charter of
the alarm-code (see [[tuba]], [[clangor]], [[ululatus]], [[hostis]]): blow the broken blast,
"et erit recordatio vestri coram Domino" — the fight-word sits inside a liturgy of being
remembered.
