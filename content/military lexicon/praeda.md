# praeda

**Booty** — the material stake of ancient war, and in Numbers 31 the object of the Bible's most elaborate spoil-law. One Latin word gathers four or five Hebrew ones.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| מַלְקוֹחַ *malqôaḥ* (31 passim); בַּז *baz* (14,31); שָׁלָל *šālāl* / בזז *bāzaz* (31,53); טֶרֶף *ṭerep̄* "prey" (23,24); שְׁבִי *šəbî* "captives" (21,1!) | προνομή / σκῦλα; θήρα (23,24) | — | **praeda** | 9 in Numbers (full text, verified; *praedixerat* 26,65 = praedico, excluded) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,1]] the king of Arad "duxit ex eo **praedam**" — the Hebrew says he took **captives** (*šəbî*): persons become plunder, the exact mirror of 14,3 where booty became *captivi* (see [[captivus]]). At [[Liber Numerorum/14|Nm 14,31]] the children the rebels feared would be *praedae hostibus* are the ones God will bring in. The lioness of Balaam's oracle rises "donec devoret **praedam**" (23,24, Hebrew *ṭerep̄*). Then the great spoil-chapter, [[Liber Numerorum/31|Nm 31]]: *praeda* runs through the capture (v. 11), the purification of everything taken (v. 20), the census of the spoil and its division between combatants and community (vv. 27.32), down to the officers' gold (vv. 50.53). The levy owed to God from it (Hebrew *meḵes*) is rendered simply as a *pars Domino* — the Roman fiscal word *tributum* is never used: the spoil-tax stays theological, not imperial.
