# interficere

To **kill** — plain, juridically neutral Latin. Its eleven occurrences include the single most consequential rendering in the book's divine-war lexicon.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| חרם — *ḥāram* "devote to destruction" (21,3); הרג *hārag*, מות *mût*, אכל *ʾākal* (16,35), ירשׁ *yāraš* (32,39; 33,55), רצח *rāṣaḥ* (35,26) | 21,3 ἀνεθεμάτισεν; elsewhere ἀποκτείνω / θανατόω | — | **interficere / interfector** | 11 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

The load-bearing verse is [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,3]]: Israel **ḥāram**-ed the cities of Arad — the LXX keeps the sacral verb (ἀνεθεμάτισεν), the Old Latin followed it — and Jerome writes "*quem ille **interfecit** subversis urbibus eius; et vocavit nomen loci illius Horma, id est **anathema***". The **act** of the ban is de-sacralised into ordinary killing; the sacral **name** is kept for the place (see [[anathema]], [[delere]]). Around it: the fire that "devoured" Korah's men *interfecit* (16,35); the kill-order on the Midianite males and boys (31,8.17); Moses' death-wish (11,15). Two further shifts deserve note: at [[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32,39]] and [[Liber Numerorum/33|Nm 33,55]] the Hebrew verb is *yāraš*, "to **dispossess**" — and the Vulgate hardens dispossession into killing (*interfecto Amorrhaeo*; "sin autem nolueritis **interficere** habitatores terrae…"). In the homicide law, the *rōṣēaḥ* becomes the **interfector** (35,26), partner of the *percussor*. What Hebrew text Jerome read is unknown; the alignments are ours.
