# congregatio

The third face of *ʿēdâ*: neither the physical mass ([[multitudo]]) nor the official organ ([[synagoga]]), but the community as a body **constituted by being gathered** — at the census that makes Israel a *summa*, and at the cultic-juridical scenes where it stands "before the Lord." *Congregatio* is also the rendering with the longest afterlife: it is the term that opens toward the ecclesiological reading of the assembly.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| עֵדָה — *ʿēdâ*, "the assembled community," in its constitutive/cultic sense; also the verbal root (1,18: *congregaverunt*) | συναγωγή (uniform) | — | **congregatio** (marked; contrast the default [[multitudo]]) | 4 nominal loci documented, + 1 verbal (below) |

*Congregatio* names the whole community as a single constituted body, not a headcount of individuals: the census of [[Liber Numerorum/01|Nm 1,2]] totals the "*summa universae congregationis*," and the verb of v. 18 (*congregaverunt*) enacts the same idea — the tribes are *made into* a congregation by the act of assembling them. The other loci are cultic-juridical: at [[Liber Numerorum/16|Nm 16,16.21]] the confrontation with Korah's band unfolds as "*omnis congregatio tua*" and "*de medio congregationis huius*," and at Vg [[Liber Numerorum/16|Nm 16,40]] (= TM 17,5) it is again the *ʿēdâ* of Korah, now named *congregatio*, that receives the warning inscribed on the censers. As Jerome's later exegetical usage makes explicit (cf. his comment on Isaiah, distinguishing *congregare* — proper to non-rational creatures too — from *convocare*, proper to rational beings alone), *congregatio* is the term left most open to a spiritual, corporate reading: alongside [[synagoga]], increasingly marked as the assembly of the Jews, *congregatio* is available to the *multitudo vocatorum* — the community of those called — feeding directly into the Vulgate's and the Christian tradition's later *ecclesia*.
