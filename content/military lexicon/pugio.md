# pugio

The **dagger** — the Roman soldier's sidearm, worn at the belt beside the *gladius* (and,
in Roman memory, the assassin's and executioner's blade). One occurrence in Numbers, at the
climax of the Baal-Peor crisis, and it involves a deliberate change of weapon.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| רֹמַח — *rōmaḥ*, "lance" | σειρομάστης (a pike) | — | **pugio** | 1 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/25|Nm 25,7]] Phinehas rises from the assembly, "arrepto **pugione**",
and pierces the Israelite and the Midianite woman together. The Hebrew weapon is a *rōmaḥ*
— a thrusting-**lance**, which is what makes the double transfixion physically vivid — and
the LXX keeps a polearm (σειρομάστης). Jerome alone shrinks it to the *pugio*: the
close-quarters blade a man could snatch up (*arrepto*) and carry into a tent, the weapon of
summary execution rather than of the battle-line. Whether that is battlefield realism or
Roman connotation, the choice makes Phinehas' act look less like combat and more like an
execution — the reading his [[zelus]] invites. As always, the Hebrew text Jerome read is
unknown (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic); the alignment is ours.
