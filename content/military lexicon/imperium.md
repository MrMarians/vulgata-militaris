# imperium

**Command** — and not any command: in Roman constitutional language *imperium* is the
power of military command itself, the thing a proconsul holds and a tribune does not
(imperium against mere *potestas*). Seven occurrences, all for the same Hebrew idiom: "at
the **mouth** of YHWH."

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| עַל־פִּי יְהוָה — *ʿal-pî YHWH*, "according to the mouth of YHWH" | διὰ φωνῆς κυρίου (10,13); the voice/ordinance of the Lord | — | **imperium (Domini)** | 7 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

The whole prior chain stays in the register of voice and word — the LXX's φωνή, the
Targumim's *Memra*, the Peshitta's "word of his mouth." Jerome writes "**ad imperium**
Domini proficiscebantur… et ad imperium illius figebant tabernaculum"
([[Liber Numerorum/09|Nm 9,18]], twice, and again twice at 9,20) and "moverunt castra primi
**iuxta imperium** Domini in manu Mosi" ([[Liber Numerorum/10|Nm 10,13]]): the camp marches
and halts at a word of command, and the word chosen is the one that founds Roman military
authority. The Romanisation is dominant, not mechanical: at 9,23 the same idiom is *per
verbum Domini* twice in the very verse that also has *iuxta imperium eius*, and ch. 4
oscillates likewise (only 4,45 has *imperium*) — a deliberate colouring, not a system. A
Roman category with no direct Hebrew counterpart ("mouth" is not "command-power"); Jerome's
Hebrew text, unvocalized and possibly non-Masoretic, is unknown — the alignment is ours.
For the same idiom applied to the lot ("at the mouth of the lot", 26,56), see [[sors]].
