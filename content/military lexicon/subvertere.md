# subvertere

To **overthrow** — and to **subvert**. Four occurrences in which the same Latin verb topples city walls and human resolve alike: the physical and the psychological overthrow under one word.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (21,3: within the *ḥāram* rendering); נוא hifil — "to dissuade, avert the heart" (32,7.9); ירשׁ *yāraš* "dispossess" (32,21) | 32,7 διαστρέφετε τὰς διανοίας ("you pervert the minds") | — | **subvertere** | 4 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,3]] the ban on Arad is executed "**subversis** urbibus eius" — the cities overthrown, the physical face of the de-sacralised *ḥērem* (see [[interficere]], [[anathema]]). Then the striking transfer: Moses asks Gad and Ruben "cur **subvertitis** mentes filiorum Israhel?" ([[Liber Numerorum/32|Nm 32,7]], and v. 9 of the spies) — the Hebrew says "why do you **dissuade** the heart", the Greek "pervert the minds", and the Latin makes demoralization a siege-act: hearts can be *subversa* like walls. Morale, in this lexicon, is a fortification. At 32,21 the object shifts again — Israel must fight "donec **subvertat** [Dominus] inimicos suos", where the Hebrew has *yāraš*, "dispossess": the third face of that verb's hardening (with [[interficere]] and [[vastare]]). What Hebrew text Jerome read is unknown; the alignments are ours.
