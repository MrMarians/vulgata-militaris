# ariolus

The **soothsayer** — *(h)ariolus*, in Roman usage the low-grade diviner, the fortune-teller Cicero lists among the *harioli* of the marketplace with undisguised contempt. One occurrence in Numbers, produced by a remarkable act of reading.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| פְּתוֹרָה — *Pəṯôrâ*, "to Pethor" (a toponym on the Euphrates) | Φαθουρα (toponym kept) | — | **ariolum** | 1 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

[[Liber Numerorum/22|Nm 22,5]]: Balak sends messengers "ad Balaam filium Beor **ariolum**." Where the Hebrew locates the seer — "to Pethor, which is by the River" — and the LXX dutifully transliterates the place, Jerome reads the toponym as a title: *Pəṯôrâ* heard through *pātar*, "to interpret (dreams)" — a derivation with rabbinic parallels, and one he stood by, calling Balaam *ariolus* elsewhere in his own works. The effect is a job description in place of an address: Balaam enters the story already classified, not a prophet but a hired soothsayer — the premise the whole cycle ([[divinatio]] and its fee, [[augurium]] denied, the failed [[maledictio]]) will confirm. The textbook case for this index's standing caveat: we do not know what Hebrew text Jerome had before him (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic), and here the very boundary between place-name and profession depends on it.
