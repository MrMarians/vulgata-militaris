# captivus

The **captive** — war's human plunder. Two occurrences, and a curious exchange of places
with [[praeda]].

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| בַּז — *baz* "booty" (14,3!); שְׁבִית — *šəbît* "captivity" (21,29) | εἰς διαρπαγήν (14,3); αἰχμάλωτος | — | **captivus / captivitas** | 2 in Numbers (full text, verified) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/14|Nm 14,3]] the rebels fear their wives and children "ducantur
**captivi**" — but the Hebrew word is *baz*, **booty**: the Vulgate turns things back into
persons. The mirror-image occurs at 21,1, where Hebrew "captives" (*šəbî*) become Latin
*praedam* — the two words swap across the two verses (see [[praeda]]). The second
occurrence is the taunt-song over Moab ([[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21,29]]): Chemosh "dedit…
filias in **captivitatem**" — a god who hands his own daughters to the victor's chain. The
sparseness is itself a datum: Numbers narrates captive-taking (31,9: the women and children
*taken*) mostly without this word, in plain verbs of seizing.

## Loci (all occurrences in Numbers)

- [[Liber Numerorum/21|Nm 21]]: v. 29 — the taunt over Moab
