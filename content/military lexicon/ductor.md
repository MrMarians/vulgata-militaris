# ductor

The **guide of the column** — a word of Vergilian-military pedigree (*ductores Danaum*, *Aen.* II,14) for the man who leads an army through unknown country. One occurrence, and a remarkable one.

## Textual tradition

| TM (Hebrew) | LXX | Hexaplaric | Vulgate | Occurrences |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| וְהָיִיתָ לָּנוּ לְעֵינָיִם — "you shall be **eyes** for us" | καὶ ἔσῃ ἐν ἡμῖν πρεσβύτης — "you shall be an **elder** among us" | — | **eris ductor noster** | 1 in Numbers (hapax) |

At [[Liber Numerorum/10|Nm 10,31]] Moses begs Hobab the Midianite, who knows the desert camp-grounds, not to leave: in the Hebrew, "you shall be our **eyes**" — the scout as the army's organ of sight. The Greek turned him into an elder; Jerome institutionalizes the metaphor into an office: *ductor*, the column-guide of Latin military prose. A Roman technicism with no direct Hebrew counterpart — a body-metaphor became a rank. And since Jerome's Hebrew text is unknown (unvocalized, possibly non-Masoretic), the alignment is ours, not his. The verse sits tellingly beside *castra ponere* in the same breath: "tu nosti in quibus locis per desertum **castra** ponere debeamus et eris **ductor** noster."
